The specimen is a film cut from the periodic structure, perpendicular to the chosen cell axis, and laterally continuous. The double-tilt holder turns it in the fixed vertical beam, so the beam's path through the film grows as 1/cos(tilt). Tilt α goes to ±90° and β to ±70°, past what a standard holder allows (beyond ±70° the film becomes a finite flake); at high tilt the slices are made thicker (shown under the screen) to keep the simulation live. The simulated window is 1.2 nm wider than the field on every side, so tilted films have no wrap-around seams. At zero tilt, with the beam along c and one cell of thickness, the image matches the gallery's HRTEM view, which in turn correlates at r ≈ 0.93 with the paper's MacTempas images.

Physics: Kirkland's carbon scattering factors, phase grating and Fresnel propagation with a 2/3 band limit, and Debye–Waller damping of thermal vibrations, with B from a Debye model calibrated to B = 0.5 Å² at 20 °C (θD ≈ 800 K). Focal spread is averaged over 7 Gauss–Hermite foci, and beam divergence is a quasi-coherent envelope. Noise is Poisson at the set dose, seen through a detector with a 0.8 Å⁻¹ Gaussian MTF.
